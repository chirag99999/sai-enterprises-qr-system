1. Executive Summary

The product is a lightweight in-store customer engagement kiosk. A customer is invited to claim a ₹500 benefit. They can either enter their mobile number and complete the required review action to unlock the reward, or skip registration and continue to the brand’s social/community destinations without receiving the gated ₹500 voucher.

The experience must feel like a positive reward journey—not a form or survey. The kiosk should minimize friction, avoid long registration, and make the value exchange explicit before the customer shares a number.


2. Problem Statement

Food-brand stores have high-intent customers in a physical environment but often lack a simple mechanism to convert that moment into durable customer identity, first-party contactability, reviews, and repeat engagement. The product should turn a short in-store interaction into a measurable customer relationship while protecting unit economics and preventing obvious voucher abuse.


3. Goals &amp; Non-Goals


3.1 Goals


Capture a customer mobile number with a clear, valuable reason to do so.


Drive customers to the brand’s review destination before the ₹500 reward is released.


Provide optional social follow/subscribe actions without making unverifiable social actions a hard reward gate.


Issue a unique, trackable ₹500 voucher after the required completion state is reached.


Create a fast, pleasant kiosk journey designed for use while standing in-store.


Give operations a way to configure reward amount, copy, links, validity, and campaign dates.


3.2 Non-Goals


Replacing the brand’s POS or full loyalty platform in V1.


Collecting a long customer profile during the kiosk flow.


Automatically claiming or fabricating reviews on behalf of customers.


Guaranteeing that a customer actually follows Instagram/YouTube/Facebook; those actions should be tracked only where technically verifiable.

6. Screen-by-Screen Requirements


6.1 Welcome Screen

Purpose: explain the value exchange immediately and create a low-friction decision.


Primary headline: “Get ₹500 OFF your order”.


Supporting copy: “Complete 2 quick steps and unlock your ₹500 food voucher.”


Primary CTA: “GET MY ₹500”.


Secondary action: “Skip”.


Show campaign validity/terms in a compact, legible format.


Do not hide the skip path or use dark patterns.


6.2 Mobile Number Screen


Single mobile-number field with country code prefilled/configurable.


Primary CTA: “CONTINUE”.


Explain why the number is collected (voucher delivery, campaign identification, or both).


Support OTP only if required by campaign/security policy; avoid unnecessary steps in-store.


Prevent duplicate active reward claims according to campaign rules.


6.3 Review + Social Screen


Lead with the reward status: “Your ₹500 voucher is reserved. One quick thing before we unlock it.”


Primary action: open configured Google review destination (or approved review provider).


Optional actions: Instagram, YouTube, Facebook, or other brand destinations.


Social actions are engagement opportunities, not the sole hard gate for reward eligibility.


Provide a “Back” action and clear completion instruction.


6.4 Reward Unlock Screen


Celebrate completion with clear success state.


Display voucher value, voucher code, validity, and redemption conditions.


Offer “Use My ₹500” / “Show at Counter” action depending on redemption model.


Send voucher to mobile number where configured.


Prevent duplicate generation for the same eligible campaign + customer.


6.5 Skip / Social Hub


Customer can continue without providing mobile number.


Show Instagram, YouTube, Facebook, Google Review, and optionally brand website/menu.


Do not repeatedly interrupt the customer with phone capture prompts.


Clearly state that the ₹500 voucher requires the campaign’s eligible completion path.

8. Review Verification &amp; Trust Model

The product should not claim technical verification that it cannot actually perform. Many social platforms do not reliably expose a way for a kiosk to confirm that a specific customer followed/subscribed. Therefore, V1 should treat the review journey as the primary campaign completion step and support one of the following implementation models:


Model A — Direct review CTA + user confirmation: open the review link, then return to the kiosk and tap “I’ve completed my review”. This is simple but relies on customer honesty.


Model B — Provider/API verification: use an approved review platform integration if it exposes a reliable completion signal. This is stronger but more complex.


Model C — Staff-assisted validation: staff confirms the customer’s completion at the counter for high-value campaigns. Use sparingly because it adds operational friction.

Recommended V1: Model A with rate limits, one reward per mobile number per campaign, a campaign-specific redemption code, and clear audit logs. If review authenticity or fraud becomes material, move toward provider verification or staff validation.

12. Edge Cases &amp; Error Handling


Invalid mobile number → inline validation; do not advance.


OTP failure/timeout → resend and retry path; avoid locking customer out unnecessarily.


Customer closes review page → allow return without losing claim session.


Network outage → show retry state; do not falsely promise reward issuance.


Customer submits same number again → resume existing eligible claim or explain prior status.


Voucher already issued → show existing voucher rather than minting another.


Voucher expired → explain expiry and direct customer to staff/support policy.


Review link unavailable → show fallback destination and allow staff-assisted resolution.


Customer presses Skip → immediately show social/community hub; no forced registration.

14. MVP Acceptance Criteria


A customer can understand the ₹500 offer and reach mobile entry from the welcome screen in one tap.


A customer can skip mobile capture and reach the social/community hub without being blocked.


A valid mobile number creates a trackable campaign claim/session.


The review CTA opens the configured review destination.


The system does not issue the ₹500 voucher before the campaign’s configured completion state.


On successful completion, exactly one valid voucher can be issued for that customer/campaign according to rules.


The voucher can be displayed and optionally delivered to the customer’s mobile channel.


Admin can configure campaign reward amount, dates, links, stores, and terms without code changes.


Funnel events are available for reporting by campaign and store.

16. Open Product Decisions


Is ₹500 a flat discount, a percentage-equivalent voucher, or a minimum-order voucher?


What minimum order value and product exclusions apply?


Is the mobile number required only for voucher identity, or will the brand use it for future CRM/marketing with separate consent?


Should OTP be mandatory, optional, or omitted in V1?


How will review completion be verified operationally?


Is the voucher redeemed at POS, through an order system, or both?


What is the daily/total campaign budget and fraud threshold?


Which social platforms and review destinations should be shown?


Should there be a prize/“spin &amp; win” layer, and what prize pool is economically acceptable?


17. Product Recommendation

Keep V1 intentionally narrow: Mobile number → Review journey → ₹500 voucher. Make social follows/YouTube subscriptions prominent but optional. Add richer gamification, prizes, CRM personalization, and automated review verification only after the core funnel proves that the reward produces profitable incremental visits/orders.

The core product principle is: make the customer feel rewarded before, during, and after the review action; never make the kiosk feel like a data-collection form.
